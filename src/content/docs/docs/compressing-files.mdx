title: Compressing Files

Compress mode reduces file size without changing the format. A JPEG stays a JPEG, just smaller.

## How to use

1. Switch to the **Compress** tab
2. Drop files into the window or click **Add Files**
3. Pick a preset or configure custom settings
4. Click **Compress**

Compressed files are saved with a `_compressed` suffix by default. You can change this to any pattern, including EXIF-based names like `{date}_{original}`, in **Settings → Output**. See [output and naming](/docs/output-and-naming).

If the compressed output isn't smaller than the original, Picmal discards it and leaves the original untouched.

## Space saved

When a batch finishes, Picmal totals up how much space you freed and shows it in the bottom bar and the completion notification — with a comparison to put the number in context, like "Freed up 9.66 GB · about a 4K movie 🎬".

## A preset per file

Convert and Compress both have a **Preset** column. Each row shows the preset that will run on that file — the one from Settings, until you pick another for that row alone. Compress one video hard and leave another balanced in the same batch, without running two passes.

Your saved presets appear in the row menu alongside the built-ins, and the **Saving** estimate updates as soon as you change one.

To change several rows at once, tick their checkboxes — Compress has the same checkboxes as Convert, plus a **Select all videos** shortcut — and set the preset from the selection bar.

A row with its own preset shows a small pin. Pinned rows keep their preset when you change the one in Settings; delete a custom preset and the rows using it fall back to Settings.

A per-file preset changes **compression quality only**. Resize, color space, and DPI still come from Settings and apply to the whole batch.

## Before/after preview

After compressing an image, click the screen icon on any processed file to open a before/after slider. Drag it around to compare the original and compressed versions side by side — useful for dialing in quality when you're not sure how far you can push it.

## Custom settings

Pick **Custom** to dial in your own quality, codec, bitrate, and other settings per format. See [image compression](/docs/image-compression) and [audio & video](/docs/audio-and-video) for details.

### Save your own presets

Once you've tuned settings you like, click **Save as preset**, give it a name, and it appears alongside the built-in presets the next time you compress. Custom presets work for images, audio, and video, so you can keep a "client delivery" or "podcast" preset a click away instead of re-entering values each time.
