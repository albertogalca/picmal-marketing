Hello!

Another month gone. July was the busiest one Picmal has had, nine releases, and most of them added a whole new tool to the sidebar. Picmal keeps growing into that media toolkit for your Mac I keep talking about.

Let's get to it.

## What's new

### Picmal is on Raycast

The one I've been waiting to tell you about. The [Picmal extension](https://www.raycast.com/albertogalca/picmal) is live in the Raycast Store, so you can convert, compress, combine PDFs, split PDFs, build a PDF from images, merge audio, combine videos, and generate app icons without ever opening the app. Install it from the Store and that's it, no API key, no separate download. It drives the copy of Picmal you already have.

It also ships Raycast AI tools, so you can just ask: `@picmal convert these to webp`. Everything still runs locally on your Mac.

![Picmal in Raycast](/raycast.png)

### Vectorize and Remove Background

Two new image tools. Vectorize traces a PNG or JPG into a real SVG, actual paths and not a bitmap in disguise, so it shines on logos, icons, and flat art. Pick a style per image (Logo, Photo, or B&W) and trace a whole batch at once.

![Picmal's Vectorize tool with a potted plant traced into a crisp SVG](/images/changelog/1.8.0/vectorize.png)

Remove Background does what it says. Drop an image and Picmal finds the subject and drops everything else to transparent. On device, nothing uploaded, no account, and it handles a batch too. It works in Shortcuts and from the terminal as well.

![The same potted plant with its background removed, previewed on a transparent checkerboard](/images/changelog/1.8.0/remove_background.png)

### App Icons from a single image

Give Picmal one image and it hands you back a macOS `.icns`, a Windows `.ico`, and an iOS icon set you can drag straight into Xcode. No export presets to fiddle with. You can also round the macOS icon into a proper tile, Big Sur squircle or the rounder Tahoe corner, with the margin and shadow baked in.

![App Icons in Picmal: a 1024px source image with the macOS, Windows, and iOS output formats checked](/images/changelog/1.7.8/app_icons.png)

### Audiobooks

M4B files now convert to and from M4A and MP3 with chapters and cover art intact. There's a new Split by Chapter tool that breaks a long book into one tidy file per chapter. And Merge Audio can go the other way, turning a folder of loose tracks into a single M4B where each file becomes its own chapter.

![Split by Chapter in Picmal: a chaptered audiobook's chapters listed, ready to split into one file per chapter](/images/changelog/1.7.6/split_by_chapter.png)

### Combine Videos and Merge Audio

Two tools for joining things. Drop in a few clips (or a few tracks), drag them into the order you want, and get one file back. When everything already matches, Picmal copies the streams together untouched, so no re-encoding and no quality loss. Mix formats and it re-encodes to match.

![Combine Videos in Picmal: three video clips queued with the Video Options popover open](/images/changelog/1.7.7/combine_videos.png)

## What I improved

### Cap a video to a resolution

Video Options gains a Resolution setting: 2160p (4K), 1440p, 1080p, 720p, 480p, 360p, or Original. Big files can now come out smaller by size, not just codec. Picmal only ever scales down and keeps the aspect ratio, so anything already smaller than your pick is left alone. Works in Convert and Compress.

![The Video settings in Picmal with the Resolution dropdown open](/images/changelog/1.7.9/set_video_resolution.png)

### Converting with compression on now makes smaller files

Convert mode now uses the same optimized encoders as Compress mode, so JPEGs, PNGs, and GIFs come out smaller. Formats like BMP, JP2, and JXL that used to pass through untouched get compressed too.

### WebP no longer ends up bigger than the original

This was a big one. With compression off, Picmal was encoding WebP (along with AVIF and HEIC) at full quality, so a 355 KB JPEG could balloon past 1 MB. It now converts at a visually lossless quality that stays well under the original.

### Subtitles survive conversion

Videos carrying soft subtitle tracks used to lose them every time Picmal re-encoded the file. They now come along for the ride whenever the output format can hold them.

### A self-serve portal for your license

Lost your key, or need to free up a device? The License tab links straight to a portal that emails you a sign-in link, then lets you recover your key, move seats between machines, and manage renewals. Your license also verifies offline now, so Picmal stays activated on a plane or a flaky connection. Existing licenses carry over on their own.

### Other small things

- New PDF tools: Organize Pages (reorder and delete pages visually) and Split PDF by page range.
- Two new color space targets, Grayscale and CMYK, for black and white and print-ready output.
- Transparent images converted to JPEG now fill with white instead of black.
- Drop an `.icns` in and every size inside it comes out as its own PNG or JPG.
- Plus crash fixes for damaged HEIC files, hanging PDF compression, and that "access data from other apps" prompt on launch.
