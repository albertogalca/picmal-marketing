Every photo carries hidden data: where it was taken, what camera shot it, the date, serial numbers, and more. Picmal lets you decide exactly how much of that travels with your exports — strip it all for privacy, keep it all for your archive, or choose category by category. You can also stamp your own copyright onto every file, even while stripping everything else.

The metadata controls live in **Settings → General**, under the **Metadata** and **Copyright & ownership** sections. Whatever you pick applies to both Convert and Compress, and to images, audio, and video.

## Choosing what to keep

Pick one of four modes:

| Mode                   | What it does                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strip all metadata     | Smallest files. Removes the original's camera, date, location, and copyright info.            |
| Keep essential only    | Keeps orientation, color profile, and copyright; drops location and serial numbers.           |
| Keep all metadata      | Preserves all of the original's metadata.                                                     |
| Choose what to keep…   | Custom mode — pick exactly which categories survive (see below).                              |

Stripping metadata makes files smaller and protects your privacy. Keeping it is useful for archiving, attribution, and keeping a photo's camera and capture details intact.

## Custom: category by category

Choose **Choose what to keep…** and Picmal shows a checklist. Each category is independent, so you can (for example) drop location and serial numbers while keeping everything else:

| Category            | What it covers                                            |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Location (GPS)      | GPS coordinates of where the photo was taken              |
| Camera & exposure   | Make, model, lens, and exposure settings                  |
| Date & time taken   | When the photo was captured                               |
| Copyright & author  | Copyright notice, author, and credit                      |
| Color profile (ICC) | Embedded ICC color profile                                |
| Orientation         | Rotation tag describing how to display the image          |
| Serial & owner IDs  | Camera body / lens serial numbers and owner name          |
| Embedded thumbnail  | Small preview embedded by the original camera             |

The most common privacy combo: keep camera, date, and color profile, but uncheck **Location (GPS)** and **Serial & owner IDs** so you can share photos without leaking where you live or which camera is yours.

## Add your own copyright

Fill in the **Copyright & ownership** fields once and Picmal stamps them into every export across EXIF, IPTC, and XMP:

- **Copyright notice** — e.g. `© 2026 Jane Doe`
- **Artist / Creator**
- **Credit**
- **Contact / URL**

Your copyright is written **even when you're stripping everything else** — the camera and location data come off, your ownership tags stay on. Type it once; it's saved and reused on every export. Toggle **Add my copyright to exported images** off to keep your details on file without applying them.

## RAW photos

When you convert RAW files (NEF, CR2, ARW, RAF, DNG and more), Picmal carries your camera metadata into the output JPEG. With **Keep all metadata** the export keeps make, model, lens, exposure, date, and GPS. **Keep essential only** keeps the basics while dropping location and serial numbers.

## Check what landed

After a file finishes, click the **ⓘ** button in the Size column. It opens on **Result** with an Original / Result switch, so you can confirm at a glance that your copyright landed and the camera and location data came off — even on files that started with no metadata at all.
