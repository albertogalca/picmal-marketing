Pick the wrong image format and your page loads like it's on hotel WiFi. Pick the right one and nobody notices, which is the point. AVIF and WEBP have matured enough that ignoring them costs you real kilobytes on every page load.

## Web Image Formats

### WEBP - The Safe Default

**✅ Pros:**

- 25-35% smaller than JPEG
- Supports transparency
- Works in 95%+ of browsers
- Solid compression without visible artifacts

**❌ Cons:**

- IE doesn't support it (but who's counting)
- Bigger than AVIF
- Photoshop and Figma support it now, but some older tools still can't open it

**Best for:** Website images, e-commerce product photos, social media graphics

### AVIF - Smaller Files, More Patience

**✅ Pros:**

- 50% smaller than JPEG at comparable quality
- HDR and wide color gamut support
- Browser support at 85%+ and climbing

**❌ Cons:**

- Encoding is noticeably slow. You'll feel it on large batches.
- Tool support is still catching up
- Older browsers show nothing

**Best for:** Image-heavy sites where every kilobyte matters, progressive web apps

### JPEG - The Cockroach

**✅ Pros:**

- Opens everywhere. Your printer, your grandma's laptop, a ten-year-old Android phone.
- Small enough for photos
- Progressive loading

**❌ Cons:**

- No transparency
- Lossy only. Each re-save degrades quality like a photocopy of a photocopy.
- Bigger than WEBP and AVIF

**Best for:** Email attachments, legacy systems, anywhere you need zero compatibility risk

## Print and Professional Formats

### TIFF - Professional Standard

TIFF is what print shops expect and what photographers archive in. Lossless compression, layer support, and files large enough to fill a hard drive if you're not careful.

**Best for:** Print production, professional photography, long-term archival

### RAW - Maximum Quality

RAW formats (CR2, NEF, ARW, DNG) are the unprocessed sensor dump from your camera. Huge files, but you can push exposure and white balance around in post without the image falling apart.

**Best for:** Professional photography, heavy post-processing

## Format selection guide

### Quick decision tree

**📱 For Websites & Apps**
Use WEBP for broad compatibility, AVIF for cutting-edge performance, JPEG as fallback

**🖨️ For Print**
Use TIFF or high-quality JPEG (95%+), PNG for graphics with transparency

**📸 For Photography**
Shoot in RAW, edit in TIFF, deliver in WEBP/JPEG depending on use case
