Last month I imported 800 photos from my iPhone to my Mac. Every single one was HEIC. I needed JPGs to upload to a client's WordPress site, and the site rejected every file.

This is the problem most people hit. Your iPhone shoots HEIC by default since iOS 11. It's a great format — roughly half the size of JPG at the same quality. But the rest of the world hasn't caught up. WordPress, Shopify, email clients, Windows machines, older apps — they all choke on HEIC.

So you need to convert them. And if you have more than a handful, you need to do it in batch.

I've tried every method. Here's what actually works, ranked by how much friction each one adds to your day.

## Preview — fine for a few files

Preview is already on your Mac. It can convert images.

1. Select your HEIC files in Finder
2. Right-click → Open With → Preview
3. In Preview, press Cmd+A to select all
4. File → Export Selected Images
5. Pick JPEG, choose a destination folder

**Where it falls apart:** Preview wasn't designed for batch work. Above ~50 files it gets sluggish. It flattens your folder structure into a single directory. And it strips some metadata silently — you won't know your GPS data is gone until you need it.

Good enough for 10-20 files. Beyond that, it becomes the problem.

## Quick Actions — the hidden Finder option

Since macOS Monterey, there's a built-in option most people miss:

1. Select your HEIC files in Finder
2. Right-click → Quick Actions → Convert Image
3. Pick JPEG and a size
4. Done

It's faster than Preview for small batches and creates copies (doesn't overwrite originals). But it has the same limits: no metadata preservation, no folder structure, no quality control beyond "small/medium/large/actual size."

## Automator — powerful but fragile

You can build a reusable workflow in Automator:

1. Open Automator (it's in Applications)
2. Create a new Workflow
3. Add "Get Selected Finder Items"
4. Add "Change Type of Images" and set it to JPEG
5. Save it

Then you can select files in Finder, right-click → Services → your workflow name.

**The catch:** Automator replaces your originals unless you add a "Copy Finder Items" step first. I learned this the hard way. It also doesn't handle nested folders — everything gets dumped into one directory.

If you're comfortable with Automator, this is a decent free option for regular use. But it's one mistake away from overwriting your originals.

## Command line — for developers

If you're comfortable with Terminal:

```bash
# Install ImageMagick
brew install imagemagick

# Convert all HEIC files in current directory to JPG
for file in *.heic; do
  magick "$file" "${file%.heic}.jpg"
done

# Recursive, preserving folder structure
find . -name "*.heic" -exec sh -c 'magick "$1" "${1%.heic}.jpg"' _ {} \;
```

Fast, scriptable, handles any volume. But it's a command line tool — there's no preview, no quality slider, and you need to know what you're doing. Most people reading this post aren't looking for a Terminal solution.

## A note on online converters

You'll find dozens of "free HEIC to JPG" websites if you search for this. They all work the same way: you upload your photos to someone's server, they convert them, you download the results.

Think about what's in those photos. Location data. Faces. Your home. Your kids. Your clients' work. You're handing all of that to a server you know nothing about, with a privacy policy you'll never read.

Every method in this post runs entirely on your Mac. Your photos never leave your machine.

## The real solution to the HEIC problem

You can also tell your iPhone to stop shooting HEIC entirely. Go to Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible. Your photos will be JPG from now on.

The downside: your photos will be roughly twice the size. If you're running low on phone storage, that matters.

The better approach is to keep shooting HEIC (it's genuinely a better format) and convert when you need to share. That's the workflow Picmal was built for — keep the originals, convert copies on demand.
